# EBT-R01 — Research concept and pre-proposal (version 0.1)

**Proposed paper:** *When Surface Temperatures Mislead: Observability and Cross-Boundary Validation of Compact Thermal Models for Low-Voltage Power-Electronics Cooling.*

## 1. Research motivation

Package-surface temperatures are much easier to obtain than semiconductor junction temperatures. A compact thermal network can reproduce an observed case temperature yet give the wrong internal temperature if the sensor set does not constrain the relevant internal thermal parameters. At the system level, changing airflow, the external heatsink or the mounting interface changes the thermal boundary. A model calibrated on one assembly can therefore be predictively unreliable on another. The literature already recognizes these challenges. The intended contribution is **a particular well-controlled experimental and uncertainty-aware benchmark**, not a general discovery of them.

## 2. Formal questions and falsifiable hypotheses

- **RQ1 / H1:** Which combinations of package, sink and independent boundary measurements are sufficient to distinguish alternative internal thermal pathways over the time and power range of the intended bench? H1: package-only measurements can leave materially different junction temperatures observationally indistinguishable within stated measurement uncertainty. Test: paired alternative models, measured-output sensitivity, profile likelihood or comparable interval method, direct junction-sensitive reference **only if technically feasible**.
- **RQ2 / H2:** Does an explicit independently measured cooling boundary reduce prediction error compared with a fixed-boundary compact model in physically independent holdout conditions? Test: preregistered natural-to-forced cooling transfer, model fitted on natural condition only, full holdout, uncertainty on both measurements and boundary inputs.
- **RQ3 / H3:** How strongly do reassembly and interface changes affect predictions obtained from a single assembly? Test: randomized or blocked independent remounts, repeat measurements, hold out entire mounting events from identification, model hierarchical variation if supported.

## 3. Conceptual model

For synthetic feasibility, a linear 3-node Cauer-like passive heat path (junction/package/sink) is used. The model is implemented in `src/ebt/model.py` with an exact sampled zero-order-hold state transition. Its parameters are illustrative, not MOSFET properties. Model fit uses one package-sensor history at 5 W, natural convection. The 3 W, forced convection and altered-interface synthetic cases remain excluded from calibration.

**Preliminary synthetic evidence, generated by `scripts/run_study.py`:** see `results/metrics.json` and figures. The benchmark demonstrates a possible structural failure mode; it does NOT quantify any real MOSFET, facility sensor, or KTH laboratory capability. The boundary-aware prediction uses a separately supplied `Rsa` input known by construction in the synthetic experiment.

## 4. Proposed physical test architecture

Main DUT: selected low-voltage MOSFET and a separately characterized non-semiconductor resistive reference heater. Use a regulated, current-limited isolated DC supply, appropriate shunt/current sensing, differential voltage measurement across heat source, thermocouple/RTD sensors at defined case and sink positions, ambient logger, and a repeatable heatsink fixture. A reference junction-sensitive measurement (e.g., validated temperature-sensitive electrical parameter method) is **an optional, resource-gated work package**, not something presumed achievable with a thermal camera.

No physical connection to mains, high-energy batteries or destructive limits in the pilot. No claim of JESD51-14 compliance or qualified junction measurement without full setup and standard review.

## 5. Experimental design and validation controls

A provisional 3 x 3 x 2 configuration (power x cooling x interface) with three genuinely independent replicates creates 54 runs. This is only a planning envelope. A pilot must establish warmup time, measurement variance, mount variability, maximum safe temperature, and whether the factorial is feasible. The main holdout must consist of entire configurations/re-mounts, never randomized points from one transient.

**Primary responses:** calibrated sensor temperatures, electrical dissipation from independently measured voltage and current, dimensionless model bias normalized by combined uncertainty, transient RMSE/MAE, steady-state thermal resistance at defined reference locations, and prediction interval coverage.

**Potential secondary responses:** heat-sink transient time constant, variation across remounts, energy-balance residual with recorded heat storage, computational cost and modeling error decomposition.

## 6. Verification and uncertainty

- Check unit consistency, nonnegative passive parameters and positive dissipation.
- Test numerical time-step convergence and conservation of energy for synthetic code.
- Verify voltage, current, temperature, timestamps, electrical power and ambient measurements before campaign.
- Estimate Type A repeatability and Type B sensor/reference uncertainties; account for sensor placement, temporal lag, interface variability, supply stability, estimated convection boundaries and possible correlated errors.
- Independent calibration or qualified verification references are required for traceability claims.
- Apply ASME V&V 20 concepts for explicit *specified validation points*, not a global statement of numerical validity.

## 7. Feasibility and major risks

1. **Access:** independent experiment permission at ELAB and machine access at KTH workshop are unconfirmed.
2. **Junction data:** package thermal imaging is not direct junction thermometry; true Tj evidence needs additional measurement capability.
3. **Boundary measurement:** Rsa for real experiments cannot be read directly from fan setting; it must be independently characterized with uncertainty.
4. **Identifiability:** with slow/noisy sensors, internal parameters may remain unresolved. That is a result, not a failed dissertation.
5. **Novelty:** substantial prior art overlaps with the general premise. A peer-reviewed paper is conditional on a documented gap and results.
6. **Institutional framing:** independent research, no KTH sponsorship, authorization, review or coauthorship established.

## 8. Decision gates

**Gate 0 (complete now):** deterministic solver, test suite, synthetic holdout, code and reproducibility package.

**Gate 1:** full-text prior-art matrix; identify a defensible new contribution or select an open benchmark/data paper framing.

**Gate 2:** confirm facility access, instrument calibration evidence, TSEP feasibility (if pursued), safe hardware and resource budget.

**Gate 3:** preregistered test plan with acceptance rules, pilot estimate of uncertainty and power; independent technical pre-review.

**Gate 4:** execute enough independently replicated and held-out physical experiments to support data-grounded conclusions.

**Gate 5:** manuscript external critical review, open reproducibility audit, archival release, KTH collaboration enquiry grounded in actual results.

## 9. Academic outreach after work has matured

Present KTH with actual software, test verification results, a critical literature-gap matrix and a realistic equipment requirements list. Do not represent synthetic model results as empirical evidence. A future two-page pitch could request (1) method review, (2) technical advice on junction-sensitive measurement and (3) collaboration or equipment access if the problem is relevant.
