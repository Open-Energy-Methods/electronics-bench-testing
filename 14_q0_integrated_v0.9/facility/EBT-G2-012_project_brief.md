# Electronics Bench Testing — proposed Student Workshop project brief

**Project:** Electronics Bench Testing (EBT) · **Role:** independent, KTH-registered student seeking approved workshop access · **Initial work:** unpowered mechanical inspection and possible prototype fabrication. **Not an institutional KTH research project or authorized powered test.**

## Goal

Develop a **repeatable mechanical mounting arrangement** for a small resistive heat source and finned aluminium heatsink, with documented mounting geometry, sensor placement and reassembly repeatability. Future temperature/power measurements will take place only in a **separately approved electrical test setting**.

The research examines whether a thermal boundary characterized independently predicts observable package/surface temperatures when cooling or heat-source location changes. Current results are numerical and synthetic; the mechanical fixture has not been manufactured or tested.

## Bounded mechanical deliverables

- **First:** inspect actual parts when obtained; record the heatsink's real mating face, overall geometry and usable mounting envelope with suitable instruments. Keep supplier/catalog dimensions separate from physical readings.
- **Then, subject to staff review:** choose direct-to-sink mounting versus a justified optional intermediate plate; design a removable sensor support and strain relief. Produce a revisioned *review drawing* with dimensions from the as-received part and named material/fasteners.
- **If allowed:** obtain machine-specific training, staff approval of operation/risk assessment and fabricate only the approved mechanical prototype. Inspect and document it unpowered.
- **Handover:** a physical assembly revision, component list, dimensional inspection notes and photos/data for which publication is permitted.

## Components (planned, not received/approved)

- Fischer SK 92 100 SA heatsink, nominal 100 × 100 × 40 mm with nominal 10 mm base.
- Two Bourns PWR220T-20-10R0F nominal 10 Ω resistors, assessed separately.
- Optional aluminium interface plate, fasteners, removable clamps and temperature-sensor holders **to be specified after actual inspection**.

No machining drawing, hole coordinates, tapping depth, clamping torque, safety envelope or manufacturing sequence is released.

## Timeline, defined as stage-dependent rather than a booking claim

| Stage | Proposed output | Dependency |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 — induction | documented general workshop introduction, quiz/safety acknowledgment, agreed project/group status | actual available booking and staff acceptance |
| Stage 2 — feasibility | physical part measurement and permissible machining assessment | real received components and staff-supervised tools |
| Stage 3 — design | reviewed fixture architecture, drawings and machine-risk assessments | verified geometry, specific process acceptance |
| Stage 4 — manufacture | one inspected **unpowered** fixture revision | machine training and staff authorization |
| Stage 5 — release | inspection notes and approved photos/drawings shared only as allowed | review of workshop publication rules |

These are **milestones, not guaranteed dates**. The project's workshop involvement may finish at Stage 2 if no safe/appropriate manufacture route is available.

**Scope exclusion:** no energizing power resistors, no PSU/DAQ work, no heater qualification, no high-energy electrical work, and no claim that induction confers machine approval.
